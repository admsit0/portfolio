A little about me

More than a job title.

My background is engineering: a degree in Data Science and Engineering at UAM, then internships at GMV, building a RAG system over intelligence data, and at Accenture Song, building MCP servers and AI agents. What keeps pulling me in, though, is what happens inside a model, and explaining it to someone who is just starting.

That is why my two theses look inside networks, why I build Atlas, and why I spend part of the week organising workshops at GDG UAM.

What interests me

Not everything starts at a keyboard.

Understanding, not just using.

In my bachelor's thesis I measured how seven regularisation techniques change the internal activations of a CNN, not just its accuracy. My MSc thesis asks the same kind of question of LLMs: can you find where deceptive behaviour lives in the latent representations, and edit it out?

Giving an idea a shape.

Atlas is where I do this most. Instead of only explaining how a diffusion model removes noise, it shows a small CNN learning to predict that noise, on Atlas's own simulation engine. It now has 157 lessons and 26 interactive labs, in English and Spanish.

Looking outside my own field.

Not everything has to become a project. I play basketball because I like it. I also got into investing: I won the UAM Investors' League in 2025, and right now I am reading The Intelligent Investor.

Doing things with other people.

With Ibón de Mingo I have given the GDG UAM Git and GitHub workshop twice. In October 2025 it was what we wished someone had told us in first year; in September 2026 we brought real merge conflicts to solve. Most of the people in the room were first- and second-year students. This autumn, together with Héctor Tablero, we also ran the chapter's first machine learning contest.

Looking ahead

For the next year: finish the thesis, keep adding to Atlas, and keep GDG UAM running workshops for people who are just getting started. Further ahead, I want to start a non-profit.
